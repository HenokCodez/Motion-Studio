But how did they make an open-source model run this fast without melting the hardware?

The secret isn't just raw computing power.

It's a masterclass in systems engineering.

Normally, when you send a massive prompt to an AI, like an entire software codebase, the model has to process every single word through every single layer of its network before it can write the very first letter.

That reading phase eats up an enormous amount of compute.

DeepSeek solved this at the architectural level by splitting their layers in half using a causal encoder-decoder design.

The bottom layers act as a dedicated reader, while the top layers act as a writer.

When your prompt comes in, it only runs through half the layers, cutting the heavy reading work in half before the server even breaks a sweat.

On top of that, they use Wide Expert Parallelism.

Instead of forcing one GPU to carry all the weight, they distribute the AI's experts across clusters of 64 to 128 graphics cards.

Memory traffic drops by nearly 88%, allowing a single GPU to pump out massive amounts of data without choking.

And then there's the caching.

Have you ever wondered why coding agents can run for hours without lagging out?

It's because of input caching.

DeepSeek built an insane storage infrastructure using a custom distributed file system with 100 petabytes of SSD storage dedicated purely to caching context.

When you combine that with their system, their cache hit rate reaches an incredible 96%.

Most of the compute it serves has already been calculated, meaning it reuses earlier calculations instead of starting from scratch.

That is why their profit margins are sitting at 80% while their API prices remain dirt cheap.
